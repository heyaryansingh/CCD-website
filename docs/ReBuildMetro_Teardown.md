#### About Section 1 — Rebuilding Neighborhoods
> **Rebuilding Neighborhoods so Nobody has to Live Near an Abandoned House**  
> Baltimore is home to tens of thousands of abandoned properties and vacant lots, each one threatening the health and safety of nearby residents and sapping wealth from their neighborhoods. By partnering with communities and strategically working block-by-block to restore concentrations of these properties into new homes and community assets, we transform neighborhoods into inclusive, equitable, and healthy communities where families have access to high quality housing that drives generational wealth.

**Image:** Newly renovated row homes (827-833 E Chase St)

#### About Section 2 — Born in Response to Tragedy
> **Born in Response to Tragedy**  
> ReBUILD's story began with one of East Baltimore's greatest tragedies: the 2002 firebombing murder of Oliver community leader Angela Dawson and her five children. In response, the pastors and congregations of local BUILD-affiliated churches resolved to rebuild Oliver and nearby long-distressed neighborhoods in East Baltimore, so that a tragedy like this would not happen again. The church leaders connected with Reinvestment Fund, a national leader in community reinvestment, and the two organizations partnered to form TRF Development Partners to invest in fixing the blight and abandonment that had undermined the health and safety of Oliver residents and had stripped the neighborhood of resources and investments. In 2017, TRF Development Partners became ReBUILD Metro and made a permanent commitment to rebuilding Baltimore neighborhoods.

#### About Section 3 — Scale
> **Tackling the Challenge at Scale**  
> Over the last two decades, we have strategically invested over $125 million in East Baltimore, remediating over 500 vacant properties and lots into new homes and assets and reducing vacancy by over 90% in our first two reinvestment areas of Oliver/Broadway East and Greenmount West. In 2015, we expanded our model to Johnston Square, where we are now working with public, private, and community partners on a $200 million plan to transform the neighborhood into a thriving and income-diverse neighborhood that provides opportunities and changes the lives of new and legacy residents alike—and that can serve as a blueprint for Baltimore's regeneration.

## VISUAL DESIGN ANALYSIS

### Brand Identity
- **Primary Color:** Deep purple (`~#5C2D82`) — strong, authoritative, community-focused
- **Secondary Color:** Gold/amber (`~#B8971A`) — used exclusively for CTA buttons and highlights
- **Neutral:** Off-white (`#F5F5F0` approx.) for backgrounds, dark gray/black for body text
- **Typography:** Clean, modern sans-serif throughout. Bold weight for headlines. Tight letter-spacing on uppercase labels.
- **Logo:** Stylized mountain/triangle "A" mark + "ReBUILD METRO" wordmark in clean caps. Available in dark-on-light and white-on-dark variants.
- **Brand consistency:** Exceptional. Colors, fonts, and geometric triangle motifs are applied consistently across every page and section.

### Layout Patterns
- **Top Utility Bar:** Secondary navigation for transactional users (buyers/renters/residents). Light gray background. Separates from main nav visually.
- **Sticky Header:** Logo left, nav right, DONATE CTA gold button. Stays visible on scroll.
- **Full-bleed imagery:** Hero images span edge-to-edge. High quality, real photography (drone aerials, construction, community events).
- **Split panels:** Text on left with diagonal geometric cut, photo on right — used consistently as a visual motif.
- **Purple full-screen sections:** Used between content blocks as brand moments (the giant "A" logo, the colored stat sections).
- **Diagonal/angled elements:** The purple diagonal cuts and triangular shapes recur throughout as a design system element tied to the logo mark.
- **Card-based program grid:** Clean card components for programs, news articles, testimonials.
- **Animated counters:** Numbers count up on scroll — dynamic, impressive, immediately communicates scale.
- **Instagram feed integration:** Live feed embedded in footer area, keeps site feeling current.

### Photography
- **Hero:** Aerial drone photo of Baltimore rowhouse corridor — crisp, real, iconic
- **All photos appear professional-grade** — actual renovation work, community events, real residents
- **Before/after sequences** visible in program pages
- **No stock photos** — everything appears to be authentic CCD/ReBUILD Metro photography

## WHAT'S IMPRESSIVE (Steal These Ideas for CCD)

1. **Two-tone headline** ("Community-Driven Revitalization" in purple, "Without Displacement" in black bold) — sophisticated typographic technique
2. **The giant "A" logo mask section** — a clever, memorable brand moment between hero and content
3. **Sticky nav with utility bar above it** — two-tier nav that separates transactional needs from content navigation
4. **Animated impact counters** — immediately communicates scale and credibility
5. **Programs carousel** — all 5 programs on the homepage, digestible, linked
6. **Testimonials from elected officials** (Mayor Brandon Scott, MD State Senator) — massive credibility signal
7. **Real resident quotes with first names** — humanizes the impact
8. **Instagram live feed** — makes the site feel current without a dedicated blog
9. **"Whole Blocks Toolkit" guidebook** — thought leadership, positions ReBUILD as a model org
10. **Interactive impact map** (/our-impact/) — geographic demonstration of work
11. **Separate "Buy With Us" / "Rent With Us"** top-bar CTAs — meets users exactly where they are
12. **Donate button as distinct CTA in the header** — never hidden, always available
13. **AMA Baltimore Silver Award badge** — earned credibility signal
14. **Annual reports section** — transparency/donor trust
15. **Case study: Johnston Square** — one dedicated neighborhood story, deep and emotional

---

## WHAT'S LESS IMPRESSIVE / WEAKNESSES

1. **Dark hero section** — the first full-bleed photo has no text overlay at all. While beautiful, first-time visitors don't know what they're looking at until they scroll.
2. **Purple is dominant but heavy** — could feel oppressive on long pages; lacks warmth.
3. **The hamburger mega-menu** — while comprehensive, it's slightly hidden behind the "MENU" button. Users who don't click MENU miss the full navigation.
4. **No video content** on homepage — a neighborhood transformation video could be extremely powerful.
5. **Instagram images don't load** in the embed (placeholder images visible) — this is a technical issue.

## SUMMARY: WHY TRACY RECOMMENDS THIS SITE

ReBUILD Metro succeeds where CCD's current site fails by:
- Making an **immediate, powerful visual impression** with a single aerial photo
- **Clearly stating its mission** in a compelling tagline within 2 scrolls
- **Segmenting its audience** (buyers, renters, residents, donors, community partners) from the moment you land
- Using **real stories and real numbers** to build trust
- Maintaining **tight brand discipline** with a coherent color/typography system
- Showcasing **partnerships and endorsements** (elected officials, organizations)
- Keeping the site **visually current** with an Instagram feed and regular news
- Building **credibility through transparency** (reports, team page, awards)
